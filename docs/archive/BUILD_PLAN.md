# Build Plan: Experiment 1b (HotpotQA isolation) and Experiment 2 (IFBench)

## 0. Purpose and how to use this file

This is the execution spec for the next build phase, continuing the chunk numbering from Experiment 1 (which ended at Chunk 7). Work one chunk at a time. Each chunk has a goal, a build list, an explicit test list, a time and budget estimate, and a stop condition. Commit at the end of every chunk and STOP. Do not roll into the next chunk on your own. Do not auto-edit CLAUDE.md mid-chunk. The operator fires each chunk in turn after reviewing the previous one.

Two experiments are built here:

- Experiment 1b (Chunks 8 to 10): re-run the HotpotQA static arms with pure on-band sampling (100/0/0) to isolate the off-band-leakage variable. Cheap, reuses the frozen Experiment 1 difficulty table, guaranteed to land.
- Experiment 2 (Chunks 11 to 16): the IFBench generality probe on a populated, continuous, machine-verifiable frontier.

## 1. Context: what Experiment 1 found

Experiment 1 (HotpotQA distractor, 5 arms x 3 seeds, N=44, 70/15/15 band draw) returned a clean NULL. Held-out test F1 across arms spanned 0.626 to 0.639, while the within-arm seed spread reached 0.040, so seed noise dominated the between-arm differences. The frontier-band hypothesis was not supported: the frontier arm finished fourth of five, and the only paired contrast whose CI excluded zero had the frontier arm losing to the vanilla reference, a comparison confounded by optimizer machinery.

Two mechanisms were diagnosed:

1. Formatting frontier with bounded, bidirectional gains. The HotpotQA base-system F1 was sharply bimodal (about 82 percent of mass at exactly 0 or 1). The 31 genuine partials were format and granularity near-misses, so optimization had little room to move and which band you reflected on could not produce much separation.
2. Off-band leakage. The 70/15/15 draw gave every arm 30 percent off-band exposure. Static-easy made 5.3 accepts per cell despite an all-perfect target band, purely because that 30 percent fed it partial and zero instances. The arms were therefore far less differentiated than their band labels imply, which dilutes the entire cross-arm contrast.

This build attacks both: Experiment 1b removes the leakage on the same substrate (isolates mechanism 2), and Experiment 2 moves to a substrate where the frontier is populated and the feedback is actionable (addresses mechanism 1).

## 2. Framing

These are two independent feasibility probes, not a controlled cross-dataset comparison. They differ in substrate, binning, and N, so the joint output is a hypothesis about when the lever matters, generated by the pair, not a controlled demonstration. Write it up that way. The HotpotQA null stands as a result on its own. A second null on IFBench would be informative, not a failure, and the pre-registered outcomes must include it.

## 3. The lever and the design (unchanged core)

GEPA samples its reflection minibatch uniformly at random from a pool. We replace that draw with a band-biased draw from a frozen per-instance difficulty table, testing whether the partial-success frontier band is the most informative band to reflect on. Acceptance is decoupled from reflection: every arm reflects on its band-selected minibatch but accepts or rejects against a fixed shared accept-batch with strict improvement over a cached parent, so the comparison isolates reflection-set difficulty from the acceptance bar. Arms are compared on the iteration axis. The arms are random, static-easy, static-frontier, static-hard, plus a vanilla coupled-GEPA reference.

## 4. Resolved design decisions

- D1, IFBench splits. Carve our own disjoint pools (150 feedback, 20 accept-batch, 75 pareto, 300 test) with fixed seeds, matching Experiment 1. Gated on a size check (see Chunk 11): confirm at least 545 usable examples; if fewer, shrink all four pools proportionally on the 150:20:75:300 ratio and record the actual sizes. Never fall back to IFEval.
- D2, IFBench N. N = 80. Primary readout is early-curve separation and iterations-to-target. Endpoint test F1 is secondary because long N converges the arms and hides any early effect. N=80 does double duty as the main run and the drift run, which is only valid because the primary readout is early, not endpoint. If the drift signal at 80 is small, report that honestly: small drift means a small dynamic-extension payoff.
- D3, continuity gate (Chunk 13 go/no-go). Rank terciles stand if the middle tercile is at least 80 percent strictly-partial (0 < score < 1) and less than 50 percent of total score mass sits at exact 0 or 1. Otherwise fall back to value-based bins. NO-GO if the frontier band has fewer than 20 instances. A NO-GO is the signal the dataset was the coarse variant after all.
- D4, drift re-score (temperature-controlled). The frozen table is scored at the run temperature (0.6) and defines the bands. The drift measurement must not correlate that 0.6 table against a temp-0 re-score, because that mixes policy drift with the temperature change. Instead, re-score both the base/seed candidate and each cell's final best candidate at temp 0, and compute the Spearman rank correlation between the temp-0 base scoring and the temp-0 final scoring over all instances. Also report the frontier-leaving rate computed temperature-consistently (bin the temp-0 base scores with the same rule, then measure how many of those frontier instances leave the middle band under the temp-0 final scores). One extra deterministic pass on the base candidate, negligible cost.
- D5, HotpotQA 100/0/0 scope. Reuse Experiment 1's random and vanilla cells unchanged (neither touches the band sampler), re-run only the 3 static arms x 3 seeds = 9 cells at N=44, and add a regression test proving the random sampler is byte-identical under 70/15/15 and 100/0/0 so the reuse is sound rather than assumed.

- Accept rule: strict improvement (>) of candidate over cached parent on the fixed 20-example accept-batch.
- Task model: together_ai/Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct-Turbo, temperature 0.6, thinking off.
- Reflection model: gpt-4.1-mini. Reflection meta-prompt stays stock.

New deviation introduced here: pure on-band 100/0/0 sampling as the isolation variant.

## 7. The chunked build plan

### Chunk 8: pure on-band sampling mode

Goal: add a 100/0/0 (100 percent target band, 0 percent off-band) draw mode to the band sampler, default unchanged.

Build:
- Add a config parameter to the band sampler controlling the on-band and off-band split. Keep 70/15/15 as the default so all Experiment 1 behavior and tests are unchanged. Add the mode where target-band weight is 1.0 and each off-band weight is 0.0. Do not touch the random path (stays uniform-over-ids) or the vanilla path.
- Handle the all-perfect-minibatch edge case: under 100/0/0, static-easy on HotpotQA draws only from the all-F1==1 band, so the minibatch can contain zero correctable examples. The accept and reflect path must record this as no-improvement and continue without throwing. Add graceful handling if the current path can error.

Tests (add alongside the existing 68, all must still pass):
- static-frontier under 100/0/0: across 10000 draws every sampled id is in the frontier band; off-band fraction is exactly 0.
- static-hard under 100/0/0: every sampled id in the hard band.
- static-easy under 100/0/0: every sampled id in the easy band.
- Regression: the random arm's sampled ids are byte-identical under the 70/15/15 and 100/0/0 configs for the same fixed seed.
- All-perfect minibatch: a static-easy draw with an entirely F1==1 minibatch is handled gracefully (no exception, recorded as no-improvement or reject).
- Determinism: same seed produces identical 100/0/0 draws across runs.
- Band-smaller-than-b defensive check: document and test sane behavior if a band has fewer than b ids.
- Backward-compat: all existing 70/15/15 distribution and partition tests pass unchanged.

Time: about 20 to 30 minutes. Budget: 0 dollars (no API). Stop: commit, report pass count, STOP. No matrix.

### Chunk 12: IFBench program, feedback, and seed

Goal: a one-module DSPy program and a feedback function that uses IFBench's own verifiers.

Build:
- One-module program: a single predict over the prompt and its instructions. No retrieval, no multi-hop.
- Seed prompt for that module.
- Feedback function that calls IFBench's official constraint verifiers (do NOT reimplement the constraint checks), returns the fraction of constraints satisfied as the score, and emits the named violation list as the reflective feedback.

Tests:
- All constraints satisfied returns 1.0.
- No constraints satisfied returns 0.0.
- k of n constraints satisfied returns k/n.
- The violation list names the correct unsatisfied constraints.
- The score matches IFBench's official verifier output on a sample of held examples.
- The program runs end to end on one example.

Time: about 45 to 60 minutes. Budget: under 1 dollar (one live smoke test). Stop: commit, STOP.

### Chunk 14: IFBench matrix and drift

Goal: run the IFBench matrix under 100/0/0 with temperature-controlled drift instrumentation.

Build:

- Fresh cache-miss pilot on a slice, cost gate (auto-launch under 100 dollars), hard kill at 150 dollars before each new cell, resumable, retry and backoff. The pilot must measure cost on fresh cache misses, since IFBench's output-heavy generations are the uncertain cost input.

Store these per cell.
- Primary logged readout for the analysis is the per-iteration trajectory (for early separation and iterations-to-target), not just the endpoint.

Tests:
- 15 cell_summary.json present and complete.
- Drift fields present per cell (temp-0 base Spearman, frontier-leaving rate), computed at temp 0 on both ends.
- Provenance records sampler=100/0/0, N=80, dataset variant and sizes, git commit.
- Matched N across cells.

Time: about 3 to 5 hours wall (mostly the run; one-module IFBench is faster per eval than 3-module HotpotQA, but N is higher and outputs are longer). Budget: about 15 to 25 dollars including test evals, the two temp-0 drift passes, and the pilot. Stop: launch, confirm progress, STOP. This is the overnight job.
